Why legal search is different from a generalist placement

Law firms hiring laterally and companies building or scaling in-house legal functions both depend on reaching attorneys and general counsel who are almost never actively job-searching. I run confidential, contingent legal search — attorneys, general counsel, deputy general counsel, and legal operations leadership — using direct, discreet outreach, because the attorneys worth pursuing for a serious search are rarely browsing job boards.

How I run a legal search

Every search starts with defining the real candidate universe for the specific practice area or seat, which is often narrower than a generic database pull would suggest. From there, outreach is direct and confidential, protecting the candidate's current position while assessing genuine fit against the mandate. The rest of the process — screening, interviews, negotiation, close — runs on contingent terms with a twelve-month guarantee.

Frequently asked questions

Do you work with law firms and in-house legal departments directly, or only remotely?
Both. Search runs the same way regardless of location — direct client contact, confidential candidate outreach, and market-specific knowledge of practice-area and in-house dynamics.
What legal roles do you place?
Lateral partners and senior associates with a specific practice-area specialization, general counsel, deputy general counsel, and legal operations leadership for companies hiring in-house legal talent.
How do you protect candidate confidentiality?
Direct, discreet outreach is the default for every search, not an exception. Sitting attorneys and general counsel candidates are approached individually and confidentially — no public postings, no mass outreach that could reach their current employer.
